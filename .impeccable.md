## Design Context

### Users

The primary user is the site's owner: a computer science graduate-school entrant at the beginning of an AI and computer vision path. External visitors may include classmates, mentors, lab members, future interviewers, collaborators, and friends. The page should let them understand the owner's current stage and find verified destinations such as GitHub, the separate blog, and email without pretending that a mature research record already exists.

### Brand Personality

Honest, exploratory, precise. The voice should feel calm and self-aware rather than promotional. It may express ambition, but must distinguish current facts from future direction.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light-first liquid-glass landscape with the atmosphere of water, lensing, and daylight rather than a grid of frosted UI cards. Use one large optical surface as the site's memorable gesture: translucent depth, edge highlights, subtle refraction, and slow pointer-responsive movement. The surrounding typography and information layout must stay quiet, grounded, and deliberately asymmetrical so the result feels personally art-directed rather than generated from a glassmorphism template. Keep the palette mineral and natural—mist, ink, muted lake blue, and a restrained amber accent—never neon purple-blue. Support dark mode, reduced motion, keyboard navigation, and narrow screens. Avoid generic glass card grids, glowing gradient headlines, excessive blur, floating pill collections, fake terminal windows, skill bars, and blog-like article feeds.

### Design Principles

1. State the current stage truthfully; never invent publications, research experience, metrics, or expertise.
2. Keep this site distinct from the separate blog: link outward to writing instead of duplicating posts, notes, categories, or archives.
3. Design for growth: empty future sections should become meaningful only when real projects, research, or milestones exist.
4. Let the website itself show care through typography, responsive behavior, accessibility, and small purposeful interactions.
5. Keep maintenance low: one small data object should control current status and external destinations.
